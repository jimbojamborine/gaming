An interactive story

Was 2000 the best year in gaming?

250 top-rated games, one awkward question, and a ruler that keeps moving. Scroll to find out what happens when you try to compare a game from 1998 with one from 2023.

Data: Metacritic's all-time Metascore list, ranks 1 to 250, retrieved 19 November 2026.

↓ Scroll

1 · The list

Two hundred and fifty great games

Metacritic ranks games by the average score of professional critics. Take the top 250 and you get a narrow band: nothing scores below 91, and nothing above 99.

Each dot on the right is one game, placed by release year and Metascore. Hover over any of them.

2 · The obvious answer

Count the top 100 by year

Stack the top 100 by release year and one year wins: 2000 with eight games, including Perfect Dark, Majora's Mask, Final Fantasy IX and Chrono Cross. 2001 follows with seven.

Add 2002 and 2003 and four consecutive years supply 27 of the 100. The turn of the millennium looks like the peak of the medium. Case closed?

3 · The catch

Critics use a ruler that moves

Critics score a game against the games they have just played. A great 2000 game and a great 2023 game both earn "best of its time" scores, but they are measured against different standards.

We can see the ruler move when the same game is reviewed twice. A remaster keeps the design and changes the year. Ocarina of Time scored 99 in 1998 and 94 as a 3DS remake in 2011. Metroid Prime fell from 97 to 94 between 2002 and 2023. Breath of the Wild fell from 97 to 94 in its Switch 2 edition.

4 · Measuring the drift

About 0.18 points a year

There are six such pairs in the data. Each shows how much a game of the same quality loses by arriving later. A line through them gives 0.18 points per year.

The dashed line shows what a game that earned 99 in 1998 would score if released in each later year. By 2026 that is about 94. This is a rough estimate from only six pairs, and a bootstrap puts it anywhere from 0.14 to 0.38.

5 · The upweight

Now run the ruler backwards

Give every game 0.18 points for each year after 1996. Nothing about the games changes, only the ruler, and recent games float upward.

Thirty-one games break into the top 100, shown in orange, and 31 drop out. Red Dead Redemption 2 jumps from 11th to 2nd, and Baldur's Gate 3 from 22nd to 3rd. Grand Theft Auto VI would reach 104.5, a number a 100-point scale can't show, and one worth checking.

6 · The recount

The best year moves to 2023

Recount the adjusted top 100 and 2000 is no longer the winner. 2023 has eight games, then 2018, 2024 and 2026 with six each.

By decade, the 2000s fall from 54 games to 31, while the 2020s rise from 17 to 36. The "golden age" depends on which ruler you use.

8 · What this can't tell you

Handle with care

  • Six pairs is thin. Dropping any one of them moves the drift between 0.16 and 0.23.
  • Remasters may be marked down for being remasters. That would overstate the drift.
  • Remasters of old games get a boost. A remake of an old design is lifted by its release year, though the design is old.
  • It only sees 250 games. A game just below the 91 cutoff can never move in.
  • Page 1 data is less certain. GTA VI at #1 and the 2026 Ocarina of Time re-release come from an unchecked scrape.
  • It measures the ruler, not the influence. A game that invented a genre and one that polished it are treated alike.

At most the adjustment moves a game about 5.5 points over 30 years. It can't turn an 80 into a 100.

How this was made

The data is the first 250 entries of Metacritic's "Best games of all time" list, sorted by Metascore and retrieved on 19 November 2026. Release dates are Metacritic's own, so remasters and ports count in their own year.

The drift is a least-squares line through the origin fitted to six original-to-remaster pairs: Ocarina of Time (to the 3DS version and to the 2026 re-release), Metroid Prime, Breath of the Wild, Tears of the Kingdom and The Last of Us. The adjusted score is raw score + drift × (release year − reference year). Everything is calculated in your browser from the data embedded in this page.

This is a thought experiment, not a ranking you should trust. Metacritic is not affiliated with this page.
